CEDDet: A Cloud–Edge Collaborative System for Real-Time Driver Distraction Detection in Low-Resource Scenarios

Abstract

Road safety is a major global concern, and distracted driving has become a significant factor affecting it. Timely detection and warning of driver distraction are therefore essential. However, scarce labeled data hinders the practical application of distraction detection models, while limited edge-device resources restrict the deployment of zero-shot Vision-Language Models (VLMs). To address these challenges, we propose \textbf{CEDDet}, a \textbf{C}loud-\textbf{E}dge collaborative system for real-time Driver \textbf{D}istraction \textbf{Det}ection in low-resource scenarios. On the edge, we design a lightweight distraction frame mining module for event-level keyframe selection. High-value candidate frames are uploaded to the cloud, which reduces transmission costs and alleviates cloud-side processing. In the cloud, we introduce an automated annotation pipeline that integrates a strong pose detector with a Vision-Language Model (VLM). It annotates the uploaded key frames, thus constructing a high-quality training dataset without any human involvement. Using the automatically built dataset, CEDDet performs closed-loop iterative training to produce a lightweight YOLO11 detector. The weights are periodically sent back to the edge for real-time inference, while large models remain cloud-only as offline auxiliary tools. Experiments show that CEDDet outperforms zero-shot inference from multiple open-source VLMs across all metrics while meeting real-time device constraints. Even when the data distribution shifts, iterative closed-loop training maintains stable and usable detection performance, demonstrating strong generalization and transferability.
